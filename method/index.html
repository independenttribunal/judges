Method

How the record is built

Everything here comes from documents the state publishes. Nothing is recalled from memory or guessed. Where a source does not exist, we say so.

Sources

  • Rosters from judiciary.uk, checked daily, with a dated copy kept each time. When a name drops off a list, the copies before and after are the evidence. Open Government Licence.
  • Judgments from Find Case Law at The National Archives, checked daily, including judgments published months after they were handed down and later corrections to published text. Open Justice Licence. Today the database holds the Court of Appeal (Criminal Division) from 2003; other courts follow.
  • Conduct statements from the Judicial Conduct Investigations Office. The JCIO deletes them after one year, or five for removals. We keep them.

The Crown Court is invisible

Crown Court judges pass most prison sentences in England and Wales, and none of their decisions are published. The only public trace is an appeal: when the Court of Appeal names the judge below. So a judge's record here can only ever show the cases that were appealed, which is a small and unrepresentative share. We will not present it as anything else.

Finding the right judge

Judgments name judges inconsistently: "HHJ Smith", "the Recorder", "the judge". A name is matched to a person only when it is unambiguous on the rosters. Surnames shared by more than one sitting judge are never matched automatically. Every match is then checked by a person against the judgment before it is published.

Measuring fairly

A raw count misleads. A judge who sentences more cases will have more appeals; an old judgment has had longer to be cited; a senior court is cited because it binds. Any figure we publish states its denominator, is compared within like courts and periods, and has its method fixed before the results are computed.

What we do not publish

Home addresses, family members, photographs, health. Names of defendants, victims and litigants, beyond what the case citation itself contains. Anything a source does not say.

Corrections

If something here is wrong, tell us with the source: corrections and sources. Corrections are made in the open, with the date and the reason, and the earlier version is not silently erased.
