Who is judging
the judges?

Judges exercise state power over liberty, property and family life. England and Wales keeps no systematic public record of how each of them uses it. This database builds that record from published sources, and every line points back to its source.

What is published today. The official roster of judges, exactly as the judiciary publishes it, and counts of everything we hold. Records about an individual judge (appeals from their decisions, sentences increased or reduced, citations of their judgments) are published only after a person has checked each one against the judgment itself. We have 6,743 machine-extracted references to judges awaiting that check; 0 are verified so far.

What the state does not publish

  • Recorders

    No published list. Fee-paid Crown Court judges who run trials and pass custodial sentences.Route to the data: FOI to the Judicial Office; JAC appointment announcements; The Gazette

  • Deputy District Judges

    No published list. Fee-paid.Route to the data: FOI to the Judicial Office

  • Crown Court judgments

    Not published anywhere. Recoverable only where a case is appealed and the Court of Appeal names the judge below.Route to the data: EWCA Crim full-text search; court observation; transcript purchase

  • Crown Court sitting records

    Cause lists are published daily and deleted daily. Not archived by anyone.Route to the data: HMCTS listing feed request; daily capture

  • Judicial interests

    England and Wales has no register of judicial interests. Scotland publishes recusals only.Route to the data: FOI; case-by-case from judgments

  • Judicial gifts and hospitality

    No published register for the judiciary of England and Wales. Parliament publishes one, councils are required to, and the NI Department of Justice does.Route to the data: FOI to the Judicial Office

  • Fee-paid sitting counts

    Daily rates are published; the number of days each fee-paid judge sits is not, so their earnings cannot be computed.Route to the data: FOI to HMCTS

  • Post-retirement earnings

    Arbitration and inquiry work by former judges. Inquiry fees appear in inquiry accounts; arbitration is private.Route to the data: Inquiry annual accounts; Companies House directorships

  • JCIO disciplinary statements

    Published then DELETED: one year for sanctions below removal, five years for removals. The public record of judicial misconduct has a one-year memory. Nobody archives it.Route to the data: Mirror complaints.judicialconduct.gov.uk now; request deleted statements by email

  • Pupillage supervisors

    A real one-to-one relationship, but pupillage records are held by the Inns and BSB and are not public.Route to the data: Who's Who; obituaries; interviews; Inn records by request

Rules we hold ourselves to

A source for every line

Every fact links to the judgment, list or statement it came from.

Public office only

What judges do in office. No homes, families or photographs.

Checked before published

A machine can find a name. A person confirms it is the right judge before it appears.

Same rules for every judge

Every judge is measured the same way, whatever the result. A record that only supports one view is not a record.

Parties stay anonymous

Defendants and litigants are not the subject. Judges are.

Corrections are logged

Errors are fixed in the open, with the date and the reason.
